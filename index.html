Anti-scam guide

How to verify any maritime institute — including us.

Fraudulent institutes thrive on urgency and secrecy. A genuine one will hand you documents and say, “Take your time.” Use this checklist before paying anyone, anywhere.

Ask us these questions on WhatsApp
  1. Ask for the approval letter & number.
    Then cross-check it yourself on the regulator’s official website — not a link the institute sends you.
  2. Visit the campus unannounced.
    See the classrooms, labs and simulators. Talk to current students without staff present.
  3. Speak to alumni directly.
    Ask what they paid, what was promised and what actually happened.
  4. Get everything in writing.
    Fee breakup, receipts, batch dates, and a refund policy — before payment, not after.
  5. Verify “placement records”.
    Ask for joining letters or CDC copies (with personal data masked), not photos on a wall.

Walk away if anyone “guarantees” a job, visa or salary, pressures you to pay today, or refuses to share approval documents in writing.

Seafarer roles require a medical fitness certificate from an approved doctor, covering eyesight, colour vision, hearing and general fitness. Cruise hospitality roles have their own employer medical checks. Get tested early — it is the kindest way to avoid spending money on a path that medicals may close. Merchant Navy crews operate cargo, tanker and container ships — technical deck and engine careers regulated under national maritime rules and STCW conventions. Cruise lines are floating hotels — service, food, housekeeping and guest-facing roles with international brands. Both involve contracts at sea; the skills, entry routes and lifestyles differ completely.
